# 0.2.0 plan audit — after the collapse, does this plan reach the northstar?

**Audited 2026-09-05 on `milestone/0.2.0-the-conveyor` at `815de59`. This is an audit of a PLAN,
not of code.** Lens: the northstar the collapse commit installed — *a simple local Jira: it
creates the work, moves it, expresses what the states are and what the flow is, and it infers
nothing* — plus hard rules 4, 5 and 9.

Read: `milestone.md` (whole), all 14 feature records' frontmatter and the five phase-6 records in
full, `decisions.md`, `docs/design/state-categories.md` (whole), both prior reviews, the ten
review records carrying the 36 open findings, and enough of `src/agentic_sdlc/repo/` to check the
claims below. Ran `pm status`, `check pm`, `pm --help`.

**The collapse was right and the design is right.** What follows is about whether the plan as it
now stands can be handed to a builder and reach the northstar. Three things say not yet: the
milestone's own ship criteria still assert the premise the collapse rejected, the architecture the
whole thing rests on has no grain that builds it, and the sequencing worry that prompted this
audit is aimed at the wrong object.

---

## The question I was asked first, and why it is the wrong binary

> *"Phase 6 changes the state model underneath phases 3–5, so landing the 22 sound-premise
> findings first means touching some of that code twice. The alternative is building phase 6
> first and landing the findings onto the final shape."*

**I dispositioned all 36 findings against phase 6 before answering, and the binary dissolves: 30
of them do not care which order you pick.** The partition, one line each, is in the appendix. The
counts:

| pile | n | what it means for order |
|---|---|---|
| **independent** — phase 6 never touches the code | 25 | land continuously, starting now |
| **bookkeeping** — stale disposition, wrong commit credited, empty section | 5 | close today, zero code |
| **owned by phase 6** — same edit either way | 4 | do not touch until phase 6 |
| **half-dissolves** — the halt goes, the defect does not (R1) | 1 | re-scope, see Q5 |
| **dissolves outright** | 1 | close free |

Five of the 25 independent findings are BLOCKERs — G4, I1, K1, T1, R3 — and every one is a hard
rule 4 defect: a gate that prints PASS over something it did not measure. **T1 is D1's accepted
cost arriving exactly as D1 predicted it would.** Those are the findings this tree should be
embarrassed to carry for another week, and phase 6 has nothing to do with any of them.

So the ordering that was hard to choose governs four findings. It is not the decision worth
optimising. **The decision worth optimising is Q3**, below, and it is about a whole unbuilt
feature rather than about findings at all.

## Q1 — BLOCKER. The milestone's ship criteria still assert the premise the collapse rejected

`milestone.md` ship criterion 3, unchanged since before the collapse:

> *"`release` and `adopt` both run as step lists that **refuse to advance**, and a **skip** is a
> ledger row with a reason rather than a silence."*

Phase 6's `the-belts-report-they-do-not-refuse` deletes both halves. Its own scope table:

> *"`close story`, `close feature`, `release`, `adopt` — **stop blocking.** … `--skip <step>
> --reason` — **shrinks or goes.** It exists to escape a refusal; with nothing to escape it is
> ceremony."*

A belt either refuses to advance or it does not. Criterion 10 carries the same clause a second
time — *"every step that had to be skipped is in the ledger with its reason"* — and if `--skip`
goes, that sentence has no referent.

**This is P1's exact shape, one document later.** P1 was a criterion written before a ruling and
never revisited; the collapse commit absorbed the ruling into the milestone's prose and left the
criteria beneath it untouched. The plan review caught it in the milestone that was deleted, and it
survived into the milestone that absorbed it.

It is not only the criteria. Three grains carry the rejected premise **in their names and
summaries**, and names are contracts here:

| grain | what its name asserts |
|---|---|
| `0.2.0/the-belts-refuse-to-advance` (phase 3, `building`) | the premise, in the id |
| `0.2.0/the-release-is-a-conveyor` summary | *"a resumable step machine that refuses to advance"* |
| `the-inner-levels-are-belts-too/stories/01-closing-a-story-is-a-belt.md` | body says refuse |

`grep -l "refuse[s]* to advance"` also hits `src/agentic_sdlc/repo/install.py` — so the phrase is
inside an **installed** artifact and reaches consumers on the pin bump.

`the-belts-refuse-to-advance`'s *content* survives §7 intact: `pm ready-for` naming its blockers
was always the useful half, and §7 says so explicitly. It is the name that is wrong.

**And the name cannot simply be changed, which is itself the finding.** That id appears in eight
`ledger.jsonl` rows, in three of its own stories' ids, and in two other features' `depends_on`.
The ledger is the append-only durable record; rewriting those rows to make a past status change
read differently is falsifying history in the one file whose whole job is being history — the
argument `815de59` already made against `pm retire` writing a `ROADMAP.md` row for a milestone
that shipped nothing.

So the resolution splits, and only the second half is free:

| what | can it change | why |
|---|---|---|
| the `id`, and the directory | **no** | eight ledger rows, three story ids, two `depends_on` lists |
| the `name:` — the human summary `pm status` prints | **yes** | it is prose, carries no history, and is what a reader actually sees |

The five phase-6 features have **zero** ledger rows — they were created this afternoon by the
collapse — so they can be re-cut freely. That asymmetry is what makes Q4's restructure cheap and
this rename expensive, and it is worth stating as a rule: **a grain's id is fixed the moment it
moves.** `pm` has no feature-rename verb and should not grow one.

## Q2 — BLOCKER. The two verbs are the architecture, and no grain builds them

`state-categories.md` §6, the section the northstar quotes:

> ```
> move(grain, to_state)          is this transition declared? then write it.
> holds(grains, category|state)  are they all there? yes or no, and name who is not.
> ```
> *"Two verbs. Everything a belt does is a sequence of those plus commands the project named."*

Measured: `grep -rn "def move(\|def holds(" src/` returns exactly one hit,
`core/apply.py:176 def move(self, src, dest)` — a file mover, unrelated.

The inference census's third column names `holds(...)` as the destination for six of its ten rows,
and **no feature in phase 6 has a ship criterion that creates it.** `states-are-categories-not-words`
is scoped as a config schema plus a set of predicate rewrites; `the-belts-report-they-do-not-refuse`
is a driver subtraction. Neither builds an engine.

If phase 6 lands as written, the outcome is ten call sites that each grow their own
`category_of(status)` lookup and agree, by convention, to behave the same way. **That is a second
scoreboard with ten columns**, and hard rule 4 is the reason this repo does not accept them. The
`also_done` shim proves the failure mode is live rather than theoretical: P9 found it landed in
`ready_for.py` and not in `model.py:1155`, so `pm ready-for feature` and `check pm` D2 disagree
**today** about whether an `obe` story is finished — two call sites, one shim, already out of step.

`move` and `holds` are the fix for that, and they have to be scope.

## Q3 — MAJOR. The double-touch is phase 5, and it is a whole feature rather than four findings

This is the answer to the sequencing question.

`0.2.0/the-inner-levels-are-belts-too` is `status: planning`, **0 of 3 stories built**, and its
depends_on names the release conveyor. It builds `close story` and `close feature` as belts. Its
feature record and its story 01 both specify refusal, because they were written before §7.

Phase 6's first feature deletes refusal from **every** belt.

So building phase 5 in its current position means writing the halt into two new belts and then
deleting it — and unlike a finding, that is a feature's worth of code, tests and prose. It is also
the only place in the tree where that is true: phases 1–4 are `building`/`reviewing` and their
halting lives in `driver.py`, which 6a rewrites once regardless of what phases 1–4 did.

**The fix costs nothing: `the-belt-reports-and-finishes` moves ahead of `the-inner-levels-are-belts-too`
and the latter declares it in `depends_on`.** Both stay in phase 5; the mechanism to express the
order already exists and is already used.

This also closes I2 for free. I2 is *"2 of 28 stories closed through the belts, its own three at
planning"* — the dogfooding gap. The reason 26 stories are parked at `reviewing` is that the belts
refuse; once they report, the parked stories can be walked through the real verb, which is the only
way criterion 10 gets tested.

## Q4 — MAJOR. Phase 6's five features have no seam where the tree is green

The five as they stand: `states-are-categories-not-words` (l), `the-transitions-table-is-published-vocabulary`
(s), `init-can-append-to-a-config-it-did-not-write` (m), `the-ledger-rows-carry-categories` (m),
`the-belts-report-they-do-not-refuse` (m).

Four of those five are **one config schema**. `[pm.states.<kind>]` and `[pm.transitions.<kind>]`
are the same block, written by the same seed, materialised by the same `init`, appended into an
existing tree by the same append path, read by the same reader, and validated by the same two
refusals (*a state in no category or two; a transition naming an undeclared state*). You cannot
land the categories without the reader, the reader without `init` writing it, or `init` writing it
without the append path — P1 is the proof, and it is why the append grain exists at all.

**Four features that cannot individually close is the shape that produced 26 stories parked at
`reviewing`.** It is also the shape the scope audit exists because of.

There is a real seam, and it is not where the split currently is:

- **additive** — the declaration exists, is written, is read, is validated, is printed by
  `pm vocabulary`, and is named by `adopt`'s `config-updated`. Nothing behaves differently yet.
  The tree is green and a consumer can read their own flow for the first time.
- **behavioural** — every question stops asking by name and starts asking `holds`. This is the
  CHANGELOG entry, the D2/D5 resolution loss, and the shim deletion.

Split there and each half closes green. The fifth feature, `the-belts-report-they-do-not-refuse`,
is genuinely independent of all of it — it is a driver subtraction that touches no state word —
and that is why Q3 can move it to the front.

## Q5 — MAJOR. R1 half-dissolves, and the disposition sweep must say which half

R1 is the finding the last pass cited as the clearest case for rebuilding first: *"two release
steps with contradictory postconditions deadlocking a resumed run — two halting conditions in a
machine that halts. When nothing halts, it dissolves."*

**The deadlock dissolves. The defect does not.** Read the finding again: `findings-resolved`
(step 14) requires every review record for the milestone to be **deleted**, and the review record
proves the criterion 10 release actually happened. Deleting it also leaves `check pm` **permanently
RED on D1** — the review's own measurement — and `check pm` is in `[checks] all`.

Under report-never-refuse the run reaches step 21 instead of stopping at step 5. It also walks
past two warnings and still tells the operator to delete a record that breaks a gate in the stock
roster. **A step whose postcondition reddens a shipped gate is wrong whether or not it halts.**

So R1's disposition is not `rejected: dissolved by the rebuild`. It is `open`, re-scoped to *step
14's postcondition is wrong: findings-resolved must read the `reviewed:` pointers' dispositions,
not demand the records' absence* — which is R2's fix, arrived at from the other side. R1 and R2
are one finding and the sweep should say so.

This matters beyond R1: it is the test for every "dissolves" disposition. **Removing the halt
changes what a false postcondition costs; it does not make the postcondition true.**

## Q6 — SHOULD-FIX. "The building milestone" is still not expressible, and nothing rules on it

P4 found it and no grain answers it. `model.py:995` `building_milestones` is one line serving
D8, D9 and D10, and it asks `field_of(mfile,'status') != 'building'` — by name. Under three
categories, `in_progress` may hold several states **and several milestones**, so *"the milestone
currently being worked"* has no expression.

Rule 9 gives the answer and it costs nothing: **D8/D9/D10 report over every milestone whose status
is in `in_progress`.** A tree with three milestones in progress gets three answers, which is a true
statement about that tree. A project that wants exactly one narrows it by declaring one — the
engine does not guess which. That is `holds(milestones, in_progress)` and it is the second verb
doing its job.

Write it as a decision before phase 6's behavioural half is scoped, because the alternative — an
`active: true` frontmatter field — is a schema change, and finding that out mid-feature is how
scope hides.

## Q7 — SHOULD-FIX. The ledger migration is the plan's largest single risk and is sized `m`

P3 called it *"the hardest thing"* and *"a data migration, not a rendering change"*: the dispatch
snapshot's bucket keys are frozen literals inside JSONL rows **already written in every consumer's
tree**, consumed downstream by `report.py`'s `DISPATCH_BUCKETS`.

The plan has it as one `m` feature with no stories and no ruling. The ruling has to come first,
because the two answers have different sizes:

- **migrate** — the keys become category names; a reader has to understand both shapes forever, or
  a one-shot rewrite verb has to exist and be run by every consumer.
- **keep and extend** — the frozen keys stay, category keys land beside them, old readers keep
  working, and the milestone ships with a named, documented, dated opinion in a telemetry row.

I recommend **keep and extend**, with the frozen keys deprecated in the record shape and removed
at the next major. The row is telemetry, not the engine; a reader that must understand two shapes
forever is a worse outcome than one deprecated key, and hard rule 8 says this package cannot run a
migration in somebody else's repo anyway. But it is Chris's call and it is load-bearing on the
feature's size.

## Q8 — MINOR. Two verb-surface gaps the collapse commit found and no grain carries

- **`pm retire` knows one of three ways a milestone ends.** Named in `815de59`: shipped, collapsed,
  abandoned — only the first has a verb, and the 0.3.0 tree was removed with `git rm`. Under the
  northstar this is squarely in scope: ending work is moving work.
- **`pm decide` is routed and absent from `--help`.** It runs (`pm decide 0.2.0` answers *"decide
  needs a title"*) and no `--help` line names it. That is E2's finding, second instance, in the
  verb `decisions.md`'s own header instructs everyone to use.

## Q9 — MINOR. `pm ready-for feature`'s help text is the B1 finding, live

`pm --help` today: *"feature: every story at `reviewing`."* The verb asks `done`. B1 filed it
against `cli.py:71`; recording here that it is user-visible in the shipped help, which raises it
above a docstring nit.

## What I did NOT verify

- **I ran no gates and no tests.** `make precommit`, `make gates`, `make milestone` were not run.
  Every claim about behaviour outside `pm status`, `check pm` and `pm --help` is read from source.
- **I did not re-derive the 36 findings.** The partition in the appendix is built from each
  finding's own one-line disposition plus the full text of A1, R1, R2, R4 and I2. The other 31 are
  classified from their summaries, and a summary can mislead about which module a fix touches.
- **I did not read `steps.py`, `report.py` or `driver.py` in full.** Q2's claim that no engine verb
  exists is a grep for `def move(` / `def holds(`, which would miss a differently-named one.
- **I did not check whether G1/G2 actually landed.** `41944b0`'s subject says they did; I did not
  read the diff. The sweep marks them bookkeeping on the strength of the commit message alone.
- **No consumer tree was examined** (rule 8). Q7's account of what is in consumers' ledgers is
  inferred from the row shape, not observed.
- **I did not price any feature.** The restructure below re-cuts seams; the sizes it carries are
  the old ones re-attributed, not new estimates.
