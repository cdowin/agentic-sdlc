The two questions a feature review asks, answered first, because the findings below are all record
accuracy and none of them is either of rule 4's sins.

**Does the gate do what its criterion says?** Yes, and it is the strongest half of this change.
26 of 26 planted probes behaved exactly as the class declares — every position it claims to grade
goes red and prints `path:line 'word'`, every position it disclaims stays green, and no exemption
swallows more than its one line. The pre-existing state census was refactored underneath this
(`_string_constants` → `_graded(ANYWHERE, fstrings=True)`), which is the moment a working gate
quietly gets weaker; it did not. Over all 46 census modules the old reader and the new one yield
**identical** `(line, value)` sets — 0 modules differ — so the 0.5.0 gate grades today exactly what
it graded before. The unit tier at `c42a4a7` is **1119 passed, 3 skipped, 0 failed** in 17.0s.

**Does it commit either of rule 4's sins?** No. On the write side, every collapsed constant was
resolved to its RUNTIME value in both trees and compared: **37 constants identical, 10 changed —
and all 10 are either newly introduced (`<<ABSENT>>` before) or the deliberately deleted
`ledger.GRAIN_BUG`.** Not one constant changed value. In particular `report.KIND_ORDER` is
`('story', 'feature', 'bug')` before and after and `templates.GRAINS` is
`('milestone', 'feature', 'story', 'bug')` before and after — both feed printed lines, so a
reordering there would have been a silent rule 6 break, and neither moved. `lessons.FIELDS` is
`('grain', 'rule', 'source', 'text', 'ts')` in both trees: the `at`/`ts` fix was already in place and
this change only replaced its literals. The named numbers are right: `GIT_TIMEOUT=120`,
`NOT_ON_PATH=127`, `CANNOT_RUN=126`, `TIMED_OUT=124`, and `MS_PER_SECOND=1000` against
`int((t) * 1000)` for a duration the ledger records in milliseconds.

The four deletions are clean. `model.ROADMAP_DOC`, `verdict.CHECK_DISPOSITIONS` and
`verify/main.MAKE_PROGRAM` have **zero** references anywhere in `src/`, `tests/`, `docs/`, `tools/`,
`pm/` or the `Makefile`s — the only surviving mention of each is the sentence in the grain file that
records the deletion. `ledger.GRAIN_BUG` had exactly three readers at `c42a4a7^`
(`pm/report.py:64` and `tests/test_pm_ledger_record.py:1149-1150`) and all three were repointed at
`model.GRAIN_BUG` in the same commit. The grain says it checked; it checked.

The two NOT-landed items are legitimately scoped, and one of them I could reproduce to the digit.
The exit-2 deferral claims "88 sites": an AST census of `src/` finds the integer `2` appearing as a
bare literal inside a function body exactly **88** times, of which exactly **28** are `return 2` —
both of the grain's numbers, reproduced. Its diagnosis is also correct: `verify/rules.EXIT_CONFIG = 2`
sits in a different module from `verify/main.EXIT_OK`/`EXIT_FINDINGS`, so the `EXIT_*` family really
is split, and a real fix is one home `core/` and `repo/` can both reach — a module decision, not a
rename. Deferring that at the end of a 17-grain milestone is right. The `cli.py`/`model.py` split is
deferred whole with the better reason still: five features landed in `cli.py` today and splitting 28%
of `src/` on top of them is the grand unification the sibling feature warns against. Both are named
in writing rather than left as silences, which is what the contract asks.

  * **V1 (MINOR).** The grain says "every one of the 62 is a top-level DECLARATION named in the gate
    with the reason it is a different word." **Six are not.** They sit inside function bodies, they
    are named nowhere in `VOCABULARIES`, and no reason for them is written down anywhere; they
    survive purely because the position they occupy is ungraded. They are
    `checks/repo_hygiene.py:41` and `conveyor/steps.py:745` (`'status'` as a git subcommand),
    `pm/cli.py:3061` (`'status'` as the `pm status` verb key in the dispatch table),
    `pm/cli.py:1416` (`'grain'` as an English noun in a printed refusal),
    `pm/cli.py:2999` (`row.get('name', '')` on a retired-roadmap row) and
    `pm/ledger.py:642` (`block.get('name')` on a Claude transcript tool-use block). I checked each
    one in context: **all six are substantively legitimate homonyms and none is a missed
    substitution.** So this is a record-accuracy defect, not a code defect — but it is the shape
    rule 11 exists for, because a future session reading that sentence will believe the gate
    enumerates the whole residue and will not know six of it survives with no reason attached.
  * **V2 (MINOR).** The feature disagrees with itself about its own headline number. The commit
    subject and body say **60**; the grain's "What landed" section says **62**, twice. Measured
    against `c42a4a7`: **60** string literals in `src/` equal one of the twelve words of the three
    NEW vocabularies — which reproduces the commit exactly — plus **16** state words already covered
    by the 0.5.0 gate, for **76** total across all four. 62 reproduces nothing under any reading I
    could construct. In a feature whose entire thesis is that two spellings of one thing is a defect
    nobody can see, the count itself has two spellings.
  * **V3 (MINOR).** `tests/test_contracts.py::test_every_grain_kind_reaches_the_tables_keyed_on_it`
    is new in this changeset and is the right idea — it binds `model.FLOW_KINDS` to the tables keyed
    on it, which is precisely the "does a constant's VALUE match its reader" question the AST proof
    cannot answer. Three of its four assertions do that. The fourth,
    `self.assertIn(kind, templates.GRAINS)`, **cannot fail**: the loop is `for kind in
    model.FLOW_KINDS`, and this same commit made `templates/__init__.py:19` read
    `GRAINS = model.FLOW_KINDS`. It is `x in X for x in X`. The collapse ate the check written
    beside it. The case still earns its place on the other three; the dead assertion should go or be
    re-pointed at something that can disagree.
  * **V4 (NIT).** The class docstring's "**What this does NOT grade**, stated rather than implied"
    names two gaps and there are three. `Vocabulary.fstrings` defaults to `False`, and only the state
    row sets it `True` — so a grain kind, a frontmatter field or a row field appearing as a literal
    segment of an f-string is never graded. Probed: `f'{p}feature{q}'` planted in real source stays
    green, while the same shape with a state word goes red. The exposure is small (a vocabulary word
    has to be the whole segment, so ordinary prose like `f'a {x} feature'` was never graded either
    way), but the docstring is the thing that claims to enumerate the gaps, and this one is not in it.
  * **V5 (NIT).** The grain's `## Proof budget` says `lands in: tests/test_boundaries.py`. It landed
    in `tests/test_pm_flow.py`. The "What landed" paragraph explains why and the reason is better
    than the budget's — it is a row on the existing state census harness rather than a new family,
    which is what rule 10 asks for — but the budget line was never reconciled, so two sections of one
    grain now name two different homes for the same proof.
  * **V6 (NIT).** `NoVocabularyLiteralSurvivesOutsideItsHome.catches()` calls `_graded` directly and
    never `_survivors`, so the replayed corpus exercises the POSITION reader and never the
    `elsewhere` exemption table. A blanket exemption added to `VOCABULARIES` would pass all eleven
    corpus cases unchanged. I probed that surface by hand instead (group D below) and it is sound
    today; the observation is that the corpus does not cover it, which is the same "a reader that
    stopped reading returns an empty list too" argument `test_guard_corpus.py` is built on, one
    layer in.
  * **V7 (NIT).** Ship criterion 2 is "every integer in `src/` that is not 0, 1 or -1 is either named
    or carries a reason", and the grain reconciles only the largest bucket. Beyond the 88 disclosed
    exit-2 sites there are **13** further bare integers. I looked at all 13 and none is a magic
    number: `core/apply.py:226` and `install.py:167` are the octal masks `0o444` and `0o111` (the AST
    renders them 292 and 73), `checks/budget.py:121` and `pm/ledger.py:744` are unit tables with the
    unit sitting beside each number, and the rest are arity and slice offsets — the same category the
    exit-2 paragraph names. So the criterion is substantively met and only formally short; it is
    worth a line because the grain reads as though the numbers sweep completed apart from exit-2.

Two of those greens are the honest limit and belong in the record rather than in a finding: the gate
forces every reader to spell the word through its constant, but it catches a **second spelling of a
declared word**, never a **new word**. `row.get('at')` and `field_of(p, 'mileston')` both sail
through. That is not a hole in the gate so much as the boundary of what a closed-vocabulary census
can be; the defence against a brand-new key is that there is no longer any reason to type one.

I also ran the exemption table against the source directly: every one of the 34 exempted assignment
targets is a real top-level assignment (none is stale), and 31 of the 34 span a single line. The
three that span more — `model.DEFAULT_FLOWS` (9), `model._LIFECYCLE_CATEGORIES` (5) and
`cli.LIST_COLUMNS` (8) — are keyed by `GRAIN_*` constants rather than by literals, so the extra lines
buy nothing an attacker or a careless edit could use.
